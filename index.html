Overview

Can a unified LMM improve itself without labels?

Most unified understanding-and-generation models still depend on curated post-training supervision. This work removes that requirement and derives both understanding and generation signals from the model itself.

Problem

Unlabeled images contain rich visual structure, but standard post-training pipelines require VQA labels, preference data, captions, or external reward models.

Core idea

Split a frozen unified backbone into three LoRA roles: a Proposer, a Solver, and a Generator. Each role is trained only from internal consistency and Solver-mediated feedback.

Main result

One matched recipe improves all eight reported understanding metrics and raises GenEval by +3 percentage points on BLIP3o-8B, BAGEL, and VARGPT-v1.1.

Method

Ask, solve, and generate with internal rewards.

The framework does not add architecture-specific modules. It freezes the base model and trains lightweight role adapters with a common reward design.

01

Proposer

Samples candidate visual questions from unlabeled images and is rewarded for questions near the Solver's competence frontier.

02

Solver

Answers under multiple prompt framings, supplies Solver Token Entropy, and serves as the internal evaluator for generated images.

03

Generator

Produces candidate images and receives QA fidelity, cycle-consistency, diversity, and contradiction rewards from internal evaluation.

Training pipeline

A shared 10k-step recipe across backbones.

All runs use a 10k-image unlabeled pool, a 3:2 understanding-to-generation schedule, LoRA rank 16, and roughly 10k training steps.

1

Self-consistent understanding

The Solver answers each proposed question under N=7 prompt framings. Sample-level self-consistency and STE provide complementary difficulty signals.

2

Internal generation assessment

The Generator samples L=3 candidate images. Solver-derived QA fidelity and cycle-consistent captioning score whether generated images preserve semantic facts.

3

Asymmetric coupling

Understanding updates improve the Solver that evaluates generation, while generation updates optimize the Generator under that evolving internal evaluator.

Results

Consistent base-versus-ours gains under matched conditions.

The strongest evidence is the within-backbone delta: every base checkpoint and its self-evolved counterpart are evaluated under the same inference setting.

Mechanism evidence

Diagnostics explain where the gains come from.

The diagnostic figures show complementary understanding signals, asymmetric loop coupling, and stable reward trajectories across model families.

Qualitative results

Self-evolving training changes both answers and generated images.

Representative examples show corrections in object recognition, action understanding, and spatial reasoning, along with better color, count, and compositional fidelity in generation.
