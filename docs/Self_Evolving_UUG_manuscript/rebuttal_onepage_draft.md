We thank the reviewers for the detailed and constructive feedback. The main concern is whether our contribution is merely a loose combination of existing self-evolving understanding and generation approaches. Our intended claim is narrower: we do not claim symmetric bidirectional co-training or novelty in each primitive signal alone. Rather, the contribution is a single self-evolving recipe that (i) improves both visual understanding and image generation using only unlabeled images and no external reward model, (ii) couples the two loops through a shared Solver that serves both as learner and internal evaluator, and (iii) transfers unchanged across diffusion, flow-matching, and autoregressive unified multimodal backbones. We agree that the interaction is asymmetric in parameter flow, and we will revise the wording accordingly; our claim is shared-Solver coupling with positive transfer, not fully symmetric co-optimization.

On the coupling concern, the most direct test is whether alternating joint training is stronger than a staged pipeline in which understanding is evolved first and generation is trained afterward with frozen question-answer components. We are running this comparison now and will report it in the rebuttal/revision [ADD JOINT VS TWO-STAGE RESULT]. Independently, the current paper already shows that the full coupled system outperforms understanding-only and generation-only variants on both tasks, and that replacing the real-image pool with self-generated images only is worse, indicating that the benefit is not reducible to isolated single-loop optimization. We will revise the text to describe this as asymmetric coupling through the Solver rather than a fully bidirectional feedback loop.

For STE, our goal is to recover a difficulty signal precisely in the regime where sample-level self-consistency collapses. Importantly, STE is not a cross-prompt token-distribution consistency constraint. Self-consistency measures answer agreement across prompt framings; STE is a separate scalar uncertainty signal computed from the Solver's own greedy decoding. In the implementation, we compute full softmax entropy over only the first few generated answer tokens and use the maximum over those early tokens. The motivation is that a single high-uncertainty content token is often the decisive failure signal, while average entropy is diluted by short-answer closure tokens. We are also running a direct aggregation ablation (`max` vs `mean`) to quantify this design choice [ADD STE ABLATION RESULT]. The rolling-window normalization concern is already supported by the supplementary one-factor sweep over window sizes 64/128/256, which shows that the adopted value is stable rather than brittle.

Several technical concerns stem from missing implementation detail rather than method inconsistency, and we will clarify them explicitly. In the generation loop, the Proposer provides verification questions, but the reference answers are produced by the Solver on the real/source image; QA fidelity therefore compares generated-image answers against source-grounded Solver answers, not arbitrary Proposer outputs. The fixed prompt framings are also straightforward: the Proposer emits a free-form question string, and that same question is inserted verbatim into each of the seven Solver preambles. For cycle consistency, the exact frozen similarity backends differ only at the wrapper level: BLIP3o uses model-internal text embedding similarity with lexical fallback, while BAGEL and VARGPT use frozen CLIP-based text/image similarity. Likewise, the diversity and contradiction terms are implemented concretely: diversity is a per-candidate within-batch image-diversity reward, and contradiction is a yes/no polarity conflict penalty. We will make these definitions explicit in the revision.

Regarding answer length and prompt dependence, STE is evaluated only on the first few generated answer tokens, and the Solver is constrained to short concrete outputs, so it does not require token-by-token alignment across arbitrarily long answers. To further address this point, we will report answer-length statistics over the evaluated questions [ADD LENGTH SUMMARY].
