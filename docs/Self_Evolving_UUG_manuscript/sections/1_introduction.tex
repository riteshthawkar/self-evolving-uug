\section{Introduction}

\begin{figure*}[t]

  \caption{Overview of our self-evolving framework. We attach three lightweight LoRA adapters---\emph{Proposer}, \emph{Solver}, and \emph{Generator}---to a frozen backbone and train using only unlabeled images. In visual understanding steps (left), the \emph{Proposer} generates questions and the \emph{Solver} answers under multiple prompt perturbations; self-consistency and Solver Token Entropy (STE) provide rewards targeting the \emph{Solver}'s competence frontier. In image generation steps (right), the \emph{Generator} synthesizes images and the same \emph{Solver} evaluates them via QA fidelity and cycle-consistent captioning, coupling visual understanding quality into image generation supervision. The design is fully self-supervised and architecture-agnostic.}

\emph{Unified visual understanding and image generation} models that integrate visual comprehension and image synthesis within one architecture have advanced rapidly~\citep{chen2025januspro, wang2024emu3, qu2025tokenflow, deng2025bagel}. Shared representations create cross-task synergy: visual understanding can improve generation faithfulness, and image generation can strengthen perceptual reasoning. However, pretraining alone does not fully exploit this synergy, and substantial headroom remains for post-training improvement on both capabilities.

Most post-training pipelines rely on curated annotations, such as VQA labels for understanding, preference labels for generation, or both, making them costly and brittle. Recent self-supervised methods attempt to remove this dependency through role-based self-play~\citep{su2025unigame, han2026unicorn}, dual likelihood rewards~\citep{hong2025dualselfrewards}, internal gap exploitation~\citep{han2025internalgap}, and reinforcement-based optimization~\citep{mao2025unirl}. However, each method either introduces architectural coupling, relies on external supervision, or provides no mechanism to ensure reliable cross-paradigm transfer: UniGame~\citep{su2025unigame} inserts a perturber at a shared token interface specific to one encoder topology; SUDER~\citep{hong2025dualselfrewards} computes dual likelihood rewards whose formulation changes across generation mechanisms (autoregressive log-probabilities versus diffusion denoising scores); and UniRL~\citep{mao2025unirl} still requires ground-truth annotations for reward computation. Other methods, such as UniCorn~\citep{han2026unicorn} and Internal Gap~\citep{han2025internalgap}, are architecturally portable and self-supervised, but their evaluation signals lack multi-scale decomposition and token-level entropy to detect reward degeneracy. Portability in design does not guarantee portability in practice: no prior method demonstrates that a single, unchanged recipe yields consistent gains across all three major generation paradigms, namely autoregressive, diffusion, and flow matching (Table~\ref{tab:qualitative_comparison}).

Removing labels entirely introduces two concrete technical challenges. First, \emph{reward degeneracy}: sample-level self-consistency can collapse to zero-entropy agreement even when internal confidence is low, producing weak curriculum signals. Second, \emph{weak cross-task coupling}: visual understanding and image generation are often optimized with separate objectives, so generation quality receives little direct benefit from improving comprehension.

To bridge this gap, we propose a self-evolving framework that decomposes a frozen backbone into three LoRA-based roles~\citep{hu2022lora} (Figure~\ref{fig:intro}): a \textbf{Proposer} that generates visual questions, a \textbf{Solver} that answers and evaluates, and a \textbf{Generator} that synthesizes images. To address reward degeneracy, we introduce \emph{Solver Token Entropy} (STE), a token-level uncertainty signal that remains informative even when sample-level consistency is saturated. To address weak coupling, we let the Solver serve as an internal evaluator for generation via QA fidelity scoring and cycle-consistent captioning, so improvements in visual understanding sharpen image generation supervision over time. Importantly, the framework requires no architecture-specific modifications: we validate one self-evolving algorithm across three unified models spanning diffusion (BLIP3o~\citep{chen2025blip3o}), flow matching (BAGEL~\citep{deng2025bagel}), and autoregressive (VARGPT-v1.1~\citep{zhuang2025vargpt}) generation paradigms. The same recipe yields consistent within-backbone gains of +1.9 to +3.6 points on visual understanding benchmarks and +3\% GenEval on all three backbones, providing the first evidence that a single unsupervised self-evolving algorithm can improve both tasks across fundamentally different generation architectures.

In summary, our main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose a self-evolving training framework that improves both visual understanding and image generation of unified models using only unlabeled images, eliminating the need for human annotations, curated data, and external reward models at any stage.
    \item We introduce Solver Token Entropy (STE), a continuous difficulty measure from token-level prediction uncertainty, which resolves the degenerate-signal limitation of sample-level self-consistency and enables effective curriculum learning from a cold start.
    \item We design a multi-scale generation assessment combining QA fidelity scoring with cycle-consistent captioning, using the Solver as an internal evaluator to couple visual understanding quality directly to the image generation training signal.
    \item We demonstrate model-agnostic generality by applying one self-evolving algorithm with a matched training recipe to three unified backbones spanning diffusion, flow matching, and autoregressive generation (Table~\ref{tab:qualitative_comparison}).
\end{itemize}
