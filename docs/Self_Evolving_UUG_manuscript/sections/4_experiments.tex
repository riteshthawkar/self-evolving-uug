
\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We apply one training recipe to three unified backbones spanning major image synthesis paradigms: BLIP3o-8B~\cite{chen2025blip3o} (diffusion), BAGEL-7B~\cite{deng2025bagel} (flow matching), and VARGPT-v1.1~\cite{zhuang2025vargpt} (autoregressive). For each backbone, we attach three LoRA adapters~\cite{hu2022lora} for the Proposer, Solver, and Generator roles while keeping the base model frozen. Training uses only raw images from a 6{,}000-image pool sampled from five open datasets (COCO, SA-1B, TextVQA, GQA, and LAION-COCO), discarding all dataset-provided annotations. Our primary evidence is within-backbone base-versus-ours deltas under identical inference settings. Visual understanding is evaluated on eight scores: MMMU~\cite{yue2024mmmu}, MMBench~\cite{liu2024mmbench}, TextVQA~\cite{singh2019textvqa}, SEED-Bench~\cite{li2024seedbench}, RealWorldQA~\cite{lmmseval2024}, MM-Vet~\cite{yu2023mmvet}, and MME~\cite{fu2024mme} (reporting both perception and cognition); image generation on GenEval~\cite{ghosh2024geneval} (nine metrics total). Prior method scores are reported as context only.

Unless stated otherwise, all runs share the same hyperparameters: 10k steps with AdamW in bfloat16, learning rate $1\times10^{-6}$, LoRA rank 16 ($\alpha{=}32$, dropout $0.05$), and a 3:2 understanding-to-generation schedule. Self-consistency uses $N{=}7$ prompt perturbations, the Proposer samples $K{=}3$ candidate questions per image, and STE uses a rolling window of 128 samples. For BLIP3o, the Generator adapter includes DiT LoRA trained with reward-weighted denoising; this is still a frozen-backbone LoRA-only update, not full DiT fine-tuning.

\noindent\textbf{Base model landscape.} As shown in Tables~\ref{tab:understanding_results} and~\ref{tab:generation_results}, all three backbones achieve strong perceptual performance (MMBench ${\geq}81.0$, TextVQA ${\geq}82.0$) but exhibit characteristic weaknesses: BLIP3o-8B and BAGEL-7B lag on multi-discipline reasoning (MMMU: 50.6 and 55.3), VARGPT-v1.1 on open-ended analysis (MM-Vet: 51.9). For generation, Single Object is near saturation (${\geq}96\%$) but compositional subcategories vary widely (e.g., Position: 90\% on BLIP3o-8B vs.\ 13\% on VARGPT-v1.1). The framework must therefore improve reasoning and composition without degrading perception.

\noindent\textbf{Visual understanding improves consistently, with the largest gains on the weakest capabilities.} The largest relative gains appear on each backbone's weakest benchmark: MMMU, where all three models start lowest, improves by up to +3.5. Other reasoning-heavy metrics (MM-Vet, MME cognition) follow the same pattern, while perceptual benchmarks closer to each backbone's ceiling (MMBench, TextVQA) show smaller but consistent improvements, confirming that self-evolving signals sharpen reasoning without degrading recognition.

\noindent\textbf{Image generation gains concentrate on the hardest compositional subcategories.} The uniform +3\% overall gain masks distinct backbone-specific profiles. On BLIP3o-8B, gains concentrate on the weakest subcategories (Two Objects, Counting) while near-saturated scores are effectively unchanged. On BAGEL-7B, gains distribute more evenly across Position, Color Attribution, and Colors. On VARGPT-v1.1, Counting and Two Objects improve, while Position ($13{\to}15\%$) and Color Attribution ($21{\to}23\%$) show small gains but remain low overall, reflecting autoregressive limitations that semantic-level supervision cannot fully overcome.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-method context.} Prior self-supervised methods report 53.8 MMMU and 82\% GenEval for UniCorn~\cite{han2026unicorn}, and 80.1 MMBench and 84\% GenEval for SUDER~\cite{hong2025dualselfrewards}. Our BAGEL-7B model reaches 58.8 MMMU, 87.1 MMBench, and 85\% GenEval without external evaluators, curated data, or architecture-specific modules. Cross-paper comparisons remain noisy, so our primary evidence is the consistent base-versus-ours deltas under matched settings.

The two visual understanding signals are complementary, each capturing distinct failure modes; replacing prompt perturbations with temperature sampling also underperforms, confirming that framing variation provides a stronger robustness signal than stochastic decoding. For image generation, QA fidelity contributes more than cycle-consistency, but both are needed for best joint performance. Figure~\ref{fig:signal_analysis} corroborates these findings: STE shifts toward harder questions over training, the two signals occupy complementary difficulty regions, and both image generation rewards increase monotonically.

\subsection{Loop Coupling Analysis}
\label{sec:loop_coupling}

\input{tables/table_loop_coupling}

\begin{figure}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/loop_coupling.pdf}
  \caption{Loop coupling dynamics on BLIP3o-8B comparing coupled training against single-loop variants over 10k steps. (a)~For visual understanding, the full framework (blue) consistently outperforms visual understanding-only training (orange), with the synergy gap widening after step 4{,}000 as image generation training provides diverse visual contexts that strengthen the \emph{Solver}'s perceptual grounding. Image generation-only training (green) contributes negligibly to visual understanding. (b)~For image generation, the full framework similarly exceeds image generation-only training by a growing margin, confirming that improved visual understanding yields a more discriminative internal evaluator. The widening synergy gap in both panels demonstrates that coupling between the two loops strengthens over training rather than saturating early.}
  \label{fig:loop_coupling}
\end{figure}

\noindent\textbf{Joint training outperforms either loop in isolation.} A central design claim is that the Solver's dual role creates cross-task synergy; Table~\ref{tab:loop_ablation} and Figure~\ref{fig:loop_coupling} test this on BLIP3o-8B. Visual understanding-only training improves visual understanding but slightly degrades image generation, while image generation-only training boosts GenEval but yields only modest visual understanding gains. The full framework exceeds both single-loop variants on both tasks. Training on self-generated images only (replacing the unlabeled pool) also underperforms, confirming that real-image diversity remains important. Together, these results show that coupling is mutually reinforcing: visual understanding sharpens the Solver's image generation evaluations, while image generation provides diverse visual contexts that strengthen perceptual grounding.

\subsection{Parameter Strategy Comparison}
\label{sec:param_strategy}

\input{tables/table_param_strategy}

\noindent\textbf{LoRA is the best update strategy.} Table~\ref{tab:param_strategy} compares four strategies on BLIP3o-8B. LoRA achieves the best results across all benchmarks. QLoRA retains most gains but suffers from quantization noise in STE estimates. SFT improves over the base but exhibits optimization drift without external supervision. Full fine-tuning degrades below the base, confirming that the frozen backbone is essential for stability under purely internal signals.

For image generation, rewards increase monotonically without plateaus across all three paradigms, confirming that adapter-only optimization with a frozen backbone remains stable across both token-based and diffusion/flow generators.
