\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Models.}
We apply the framework to three unified understanding and generation models employing fundamentally different synthesis mechanisms: BLIP3o-8B~\cite{chen2025blip3o} (diffusion-based denoising), BAGEL~\cite{deng2025bagel} (flow-matching with a variational autoencoder), and VARGPT~\cite{xianwei2025vargpt} (autoregressive discrete token generation). For each model, we attach three LoRA adapters (Proposer, Solver, Generator) to the frozen backbone and train using identical hyperparameters.

\noindent\textbf{Training data.}
All training uses unlabeled images only. No captions, question-answer pairs, or human annotations are used at any stage.

\noindent\textbf{Benchmarks.}
We evaluate understanding on MMMU, MMBench, TextVQA, SEED-Bench, RealWorldQA (RWQA), MM-Vet, and both perception (MME-P) and cognition (MME-C) splits of MME. For generation, we use GenEval with compositional attribute accuracy across subcategories: Single Object, Two Objects, Counting, Colors, Position, and Color Attribution.

Three observations emerge. First, the self-evolving framework produces consistent improvement on \emph{both} understanding and generation across all three architectures. This bidirectional gain is notable because the two capabilities are optimized through separate loops with distinct reward signals. Second, the improvement magnitude and pattern differ across generation paradigms, reflecting the interplay between self-consistency signals and each architecture's inductive biases. Third, the same algorithmic configuration yields positive improvements across diffusion, flow-matching, and autoregressive models, confirming that the framework encodes no assumptions about a specific generation mechanism.

Compared to self-supervised methods, our framework achieves competitive or superior performance while demonstrating broader applicability. UniGame~\cite{su2025unigame} and SUDER~\cite{hong2025dualselfrewards} jointly optimize both capabilities but report on a single architecture (Janus-Pro-7B). UniRL~\cite{mao2025unirl} improves generation on Show-o but understanding degrades on complex reasoning. UniCorn~\cite{han2026unicorn} improves generation on BAGEL but most understanding metrics decrease. SILMM~\cite{qu2024silmm} and ILLUME~\cite{wang2024illume} improve generation but do not jointly improve understanding. An important distinction is backbone heterogeneity: competing methods each report on a single backbone, whereas our framework reports across three distinct architectures. Methods employing additional supervision (CoRL~\cite{jiang2025coreinforcement}, SRUM~\cite{jin2025srum}) are reported in the supplementary material.

\paragraph{Understanding signal ablations.}
Removing STE while retaining self-consistency alone reduces understanding performance, confirming that STE resolves the degenerate-signal limitation. Without STE, the Proposer cannot distinguish trivially easy questions from those at the competence boundary when all Solver responses agree, causing curriculum stagnation. Removing self-consistency while retaining STE alone also reduces performance, indicating that inter-framing agreement adds complementary information. Replacing prompt perturbation with temperature-based sampling yields the largest understanding degradation, confirming that semantic diversity across framings is more informative than stochastic decoding variation.

\paragraph{Generation signal ablations.}
Removing QA fidelity scoring and removing cycle-consistent captioning both degrade generation, but through different failure modes. Without QA fidelity, the Generator satisfies global coherence while failing on fine-grained attributes. Without cycle consistency, the Generator satisfies individual diagnostic questions while producing holistically incoherent scenes. Both ablations confirm the complementarity of the two scoring mechanisms.

\paragraph{Loop coupling analysis.}
Table~\ref{tab:loop_ablation} and Figure~\ref{fig:loop_coupling} isolate the interaction between the understanding and generation loops. Training only the understanding loop improves understanding but produces no generation gain. Training only the generation loop yields moderate generation improvement but no understanding gain. The full framework exceeds both single-loop configurations, confirming that the Solver's dual role creates a coupling where understanding improvements enhance the generation signal, and the generation loop exercises capabilities that reinforce understanding. Replacing external unlabeled images entirely with self-generated images from the Generator further isolates the framework's self-sufficiency: the understanding loop trains exclusively on images produced by the generation loop, closing a fully autonomous training cycle.
