\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate the self-evolving framework along three axes: whether it consistently improves both understanding and generation across architecturally distinct models (Sec.~\ref{sec:main_results}), how it compares to existing self-supervised post-training methods under equivalent supervision constraints (Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison}), and which components are essential to its effectiveness (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\noindent\textbf{Models.}
We apply the framework to three unified understanding and generation models that employ fundamentally different image synthesis mechanisms: BLIP3o-8B (diffusion-based denoising), BAGEL (flow-matching with a variational autoencoder), and VARGPT (autoregressive discrete token generation). For each model, we attach three LoRA adapters (proposer, solver, generator) to the frozen backbone with rank $r$ and train them using identical hyperparameters and training schedules.

\noindent\textbf{Training data.}
All training uses unlabeled images only. No captions, question-answer pairs, or human annotations are used at any stage. The image sources and quantities are reported per model.

\noindent\textbf{Understanding benchmarks.}
We evaluate visual understanding on MMMU, MMBench, TextVQA, SEED-Bench, RealWorldQA (RWQA), MM-Vet, and both perception (MME-P) and cognition (MME-C) splits of MME.

\noindent\textbf{Generation benchmarks.}
We evaluate image generation on GenEval (compositional attribute accuracy), DPG-Bench (dense prompt following), and WISE (open-ended generation quality).
