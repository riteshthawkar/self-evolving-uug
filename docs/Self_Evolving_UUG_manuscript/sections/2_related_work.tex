
\section{Related Work}

Recent progress on unified multimodal models (UMMs) has shifted the field from \emph{capability co-existence}, where one model can both understand and generate, toward \emph{capability co-evolution}, where one capability explicitly improves the other during post-training. The core question is no longer whether a model can perform both tasks, but whether understanding and generation become mutually reinforcing under self-supervised or weakly supervised updates. We review the main lines of work pursuing this goal, tracing how each addresses the supervision challenge and where open problems remain.

The most direct approach decomposes a single UMM into interacting roles and trains them through self-play. UniCorn instantiates a Proposer, Solver, and Judge triad, performing cognitive pattern reconstruction from self-generated interactions to improve generation without sacrificing understanding, though it relies on a dedicated judge component with its own auxiliary training objective and has been demonstrated on a single model family \citep{han2026unicorn}. Endogenous Reprompting (SEER) introduces an endogenous evaluator and reprompter loop in which RLVR first activates the model's latent evaluation ability before RLMT optimizes generation-time reasoning from model-derived rewards, meaning the self-evolving loop cannot begin without a separate reward activation phase \citep{tang2026endogenous}. UniGame frames post-training as self-adversarial alignment by inserting a lightweight perturber at the shared interface so that generation actively exposes fragile understanding regions, but this mechanism depends on architecture-specific interface access that limits portability \citep{su2025unigame}. SRUM uses understanding as an internal evaluator for generation through global-local reward decomposition, providing multi-scale corrective feedback at the cost of manually designed reward granularity levels \citep{jin2025srum}. EvoLMM is conceptually close to our approach in its proposer-solver structure and continuous self-consistency rewards over unlabeled images, but it is restricted to visual understanding and does not address generation improvement \citep{thawakar2025evolmm}. A recurring limitation of these role-based methods is that each introduces additional components (judges, perturbers, reward activators) or restricts the scope to a single modality, and none has demonstrated portability across architecturally distinct model families.

A second line of work seeks to couple understanding and generation through reconstruction-style objectives and internal-gap exploitation. UAE formalizes UMM training through an auto-encoder lens where image-to-text serves as the encoder and text-to-image serves as the decoder, optimized with staged Unified-GRPO around reconstruction fidelity; however, its encoder-decoder formulation assumes specific architectural symmetry and requires a multi-stage training protocol with supervised initialization \citep{yan2025understandinggeneration}. RecA performs resource-efficient reconstruction alignment by conditioning generation on internal understanding embeddings, improving fidelity with modest post-training cost, but requires direct access to intermediate hidden representations that are architecture-specific \citep{xie2025reconstruction}. The Internal Gap method constructs post-training data by scoring generation outputs with stronger understanding modules and then applies curriculum-style supervised fine-tuning and preference optimization, introducing dependence on both supervised data and preference annotation construction \citep{han2025internalgap}. UniMRG studies the inverse transfer direction, using generation-side auxiliary representations such as pixel, depth, and segmentation maps to strengthen understanding \citep{su2026unimrg}. These reconstruction and gap-based approaches generally assume specific architectural couplings, such as shared encoder-decoder pathways or access to internal representations, making them difficult to transfer across model families. Several also require staged supervised training that our framework avoids.

A third and increasingly popular direction casts unification as explicit reinforcement optimization over both tasks. UniRL performs iterative self-training with generated images and uses supervised fine-tuning followed by GRPO to improve understanding and generation jointly, though the supervised stage requires curated instruction data \citep{mao2025unirl}. CoRL proposes a unified reinforcement learning stage followed by task-refinement stages, reporting strong gains from joint policy optimization but relying on task-specific reward design for each refinement stage \citep{jiang2025coreinforcement}. SUDER uses dual likelihood rewards by reversing task directions, avoiding external reward models but depending on the architectural assumption that understanding and generation share a reversible likelihood interface \citep{hong2025dualselfrewards}. Skywork UniPic~2.0 presents a strong engineering variant with online reinforcement learning and progressive dual-task optimization, though its training recipe is tightly coupled to a specific system architecture \citep{wei2025skyworkunipic2}. VARGPT-v1.1 follows an iterative instruction tuning and reinforcement learning recipe for visual autoregressive unified training, requiring curated instruction data at each iteration \citep{zhuang2025vargpt}. SILMM, although primarily focused on compositional text-to-image generation, provides an important self-improvement baseline using iterative self-feedback and DPO-style optimization \citep{qu2024silmm}. ILLUME contributes a self-enhancing multimodal alignment scheme where the model self-assesses consistency between text and self-generated images \citep{wang2024illume}. A common pattern across these reinforcement-based methods is the reliance on supervised fine-tuning as an initialization or interleaved stage, as well as task-specific reward formulations that require curated data or manual design. Our framework eliminates the supervised fine-tuning requirement entirely and derives all reward signals from self-consistency and token-level entropy computed over unlabeled images.

Orthogonal to the training methods above, UnifiedReward provides a shared reward model spanning understanding and generation assessment, often serving as an enabler for downstream preference optimization pipelines \citep{wang2025unifiedreward}. While effective, this class of methods introduces dependence on reward model training data construction and cross-task reward transferability assumptions that our framework avoids by using the solver's own self-consistency as the evaluation mechanism.

Finally, a parallel body of evaluation work reveals that true bidirectional unification remains an open challenge. UniSandbox provides controlled analysis to test whether understanding truly informs generation and identifies persistent reasoning-transfer gaps \citep{niu2025unisandbox}. GapEval quantifies bidirectional coherence explicitly and reports that capability emergence and cross-modal knowledge synchronization often remain misaligned across current UMM families \citep{wang2026gapeval}. MME-Unify further broadens evaluation coverage for unified settings \citep{xie2025mmeunify}. These diagnostic studies motivate training frameworks that optimize bidirectional coupling directly rather than improving each task in isolation, which is the central aim of our approach.

Taken together, the landscape reveals a consistent tension between supervision requirements and generality. Methods that achieve strong results tend to depend on curated fine-tuning data, external reward models, architecture-specific components, or auxiliary modules that limit portability. Our framework is designed to resolve this tension: it is fully unsupervised, derives all training signals from self-consistency and token-level entropy over unlabeled images, combines multiple complementary reward components for denser optimization, and operates at the level of role decomposition rather than architecture internals. We validate this design by implementing the same algorithmic framework on three architecturally distinct unified models employing diffusion-based, flow-matching, and autoregressive generation paradigms. To structure the experimental comparison, we evaluate all methods along five axes: (1)~self-supervision purity, measuring the degree of reliance on external labels or reward models; (2)~bidirectionality, measuring whether both understanding and generation are jointly optimized; (3)~reward richness, distinguishing single-scalar from multi-component self-derived objectives; (4)~stability controls, capturing collapse prevention, curriculum, and filtering mechanisms; and (5)~architecture dependence.
