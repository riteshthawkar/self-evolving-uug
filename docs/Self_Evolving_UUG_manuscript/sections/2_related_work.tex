
\section{Related Work}

Recent progress on unified multimodal models (UMMs) has shifted the field from \emph{capability co-existence} (one model that can both understand and generate) toward \emph{capability co-evolution} (one capability explicitly improving the other during post-training). The core question is no longer whether a model can perform both tasks, but whether understanding and generation become mutually reinforcing under self-supervised or weakly supervised updates.

\paragraph{Role-based self-evolution and internal evaluators.}
A prominent direction decomposes a single UMM into interacting roles and trains them in self-play. UniCorn instantiates a Proposer, Solver, and Judge triad and performs cognitive pattern reconstruction from self-generated interactions, targeting generation gains without sacrificing understanding; however, it relies on a dedicated judge component with its own auxiliary training objective and has been demonstrated on a single model family \citep{han2026unicorn}. Endogenous Reprompting (SEER) introduces an endogenous evaluator and reprompter loop, where RLVR first activates latent evaluation ability and RLMT optimizes generation-time reasoning from model-derived rewards, though it requires a separate reward activation phase before the self-evolving loop can begin \citep{tang2026endogenous}. UniGame frames post-training as self-adversarial alignment by adding a lightweight perturber at the shared interface, making generation actively expose fragile understanding regions, but depends on architecture-specific interface access \citep{su2025unigame}. SRUM similarly uses understanding as an internal evaluator for generation with global-local reward decomposition for multi-scale corrective feedback, requiring manually designed reward granularity levels \citep{jin2025srum}. EvoLMM is conceptually close in its proposer and solver structure and uses continuous self-consistency rewards over unlabeled images, but is restricted to visual understanding and does not address generation improvement \citep{thawakar2025evolmm}. In contrast, our framework requires no separate judge or reward activation component, derives all supervision from self-consistency and token-level entropy, and jointly addresses both understanding and generation across multiple model families.

\paragraph{Generation and understanding coupling via reconstruction and internal-gap exploitation.}
A second line of work improves unification through reconstruction-style objectives and internal-gap mining. UAE formalizes UMM training through an auto-encoder lens (I2T as encoder, T2I as decoder), optimized with staged Unified-GRPO around reconstruction fidelity, though its encoder-decoder formulation assumes specific architectural symmetry and requires a multi-stage training protocol with supervised initialization \citep{yan2025understandinggeneration}. RecA performs resource-efficient reconstruction alignment by conditioning generation on internal understanding embeddings, improving fidelity with modest post-training cost, but requires direct access to intermediate hidden representations that are architecture-specific \citep{xie2025reconstruction}. The Internal Gap method constructs post-training data by scoring generation outputs with stronger understanding modules and then applies curriculum-style SFT and DPO refinement, introducing dependence on supervised fine-tuning and preference annotation construction \citep{han2025internalgap}. UniMRG studies the inverse transfer direction, using generation-side auxiliary representations such as pixel, depth, and segmentation maps to strengthen understanding \citep{su2026unimrg}. These methods generally assume specific architectural couplings or require staged supervised training phases. Our framework avoids such assumptions by operating entirely through role decomposition at the output level, requiring no access to internal representations and no supervised initialization.

\paragraph{RL-centric bidirectional optimization.}
Several methods cast unification as explicit reinforcement optimization over both tasks. UniRL performs iterative self-training with generated images and uses SFT followed by GRPO to improve understanding and generation jointly, though the SFT stage requires curated instruction data \citep{mao2025unirl}. CoRL proposes a unified RL stage followed by task-refinement RL stages, reporting strong gains from joint policy optimization, but relies on task-specific reward design for each refinement stage \citep{jiang2025coreinforcement}. SUDER uses dual likelihood rewards by reversing task directions, avoiding external reward models but depending on the architectural assumption that understanding and generation share a reversible likelihood interface \citep{hong2025dualselfrewards}. Skywork UniPic~2.0 presents a strong engineering variant with online RL and progressive dual-task reinforcement, though its training recipe is tightly coupled to a specific system architecture \citep{wei2025skyworkunipic2}. VARGPT-v1.1 follows an iterative instruction tuning and RL recipe for visual autoregressive unified training, requiring curated instruction data at each iteration \citep{zhuang2025vargpt}. SILMM, although primarily focused on compositional text-to-image generation, provides an important self-improvement baseline using iterative self-feedback and DPO-style optimization \citep{qu2024silmm}. ILLUME contributes a self-enhancing multimodal alignment scheme where the model self-assesses consistency between text and self-generated images \citep{wang2024illume}. A common characteristic of these RL-centric approaches is the reliance on supervised fine-tuning as an initialization or interleaved stage, as well as task-specific reward formulations that may require curated data or manual design. Our framework eliminates the supervised fine-tuning requirement entirely and derives all reward signals from self-consistency and token-level entropy computed over unlabeled images.

\paragraph{Reward-model-centric unification components.}
UnifiedReward provides a shared reward model spanning understanding and generation assessment and is often used as an enabler for downstream preference optimization pipelines rather than a fully autonomous self-evolving loop \citep{wang2025unifiedreward}. While effective, this class of methods introduces dependence on reward model training data construction and cross-task reward transferability assumptions. Our framework avoids external reward models entirely, using the solver's own self-consistency as the evaluation mechanism for both understanding difficulty and generation quality.

\paragraph{Benchmarks diagnosing whether unification is real.}
A parallel body of work argues that many UMMs still exhibit ``surface-level'' rather than ``deep'' unification. UniSandbox provides controlled analysis to test whether understanding truly informs generation and identifies persistent reasoning-transfer gaps \citep{niu2025unisandbox}. GapEval quantifies bidirectional coherence explicitly and reports that capability emergence and cross-modal knowledge synchronization often remain misaligned across current UMM families \citep{wang2026gapeval}. MME-Unify further broadens evaluation coverage for unified understanding+generation settings \citep{xie2025mmeunify}. These studies motivate training frameworks that optimize bidirectional coupling directly, rather than improving each task in isolation.

\paragraph{Positioning of our framework.}
Our framework is closest in spirit to role-based and RL-based self-evolving approaches, but differs in several design choices that are central to our contributions. First, while most prior methods require some form of supervised fine-tuning, curated instruction data, or external reward models, our training loop is fully unsupervised, deriving all supervision from self-consistency and token-level entropy over unlabeled images. Second, unlike methods centered on a single scalar supervision source such as reconstruction similarity or self-likelihood, our framework combines multiple self-derived signals including prompt-perturbed self-consistency, solver token entropy, question-answering fidelity scoring, and cycle-consistent captioning, enabling denser optimization in open-ended settings. Third, our training loop alternates between understanding and generation optimization in a closed cycle, where the solver serves as the internal evaluator that provides the generation training signal, directly coupling understanding capability to generation quality. Fourth, unlike post-training schemes tightly coupled to a specific architecture family, our framework is intentionally model-agnostic at the algorithmic level. We demonstrate this by implementing and validating the approach on three architecturally distinct unified models that employ fundamentally different generation mechanisms: diffusion-based denoising, flow-matching variational decoding, and autoregressive discrete-token prediction.

\paragraph{Practical comparison axes used in this paper.}
To make comparisons reproducible and fair, we evaluate prior work and our method along five axes: (1) \emph{self-supervision purity} (degree of reliance on external labels/reward models), (2) \emph{bidirectionality} (U$\leftrightarrow$G both optimized vs one-way transfer), (3) \emph{reward richness} (single vs multi-component self-derived objectives), (4) \emph{stability controls} (collapse prevention, curriculum, and filtering mechanisms), and (5) \emph{architecture dependence}. We use these axes to explain not only final performance, but also why some frameworks improve one side while leaving the understanding--generation gap largely unresolved.
